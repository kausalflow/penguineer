Finding the actual lag¶

The dead times on the plant graph are often a guess: a volume over a design flow, or an engineer's memory. penguineer lag reads the actual delay from the data, between two tags or from a tag to a lab result, and says how the material travels: with a fixed delay, a delay that follows the flow, or a delay with mixing.

penguineer lag --data historian.parquet --cause FT-102 \
  --effect product_property --lims lims.csv --flow FT-101 --flow FT-102 \
  --max-lag-minutes 150

lag: FT-102 -> product_property (lab, 476 lab samples)
flow: FT-101 + FT-102, mean 20.1 m3/h

  model   dead                      mixing                delay   corr  strength
  fixed   34.54 min [20.17, 46.64]  -                 34.54 min  +0.67  3.86x chance
  flow    10.14 m3 [7.182, 14.36]   -                 30.25 min  +0.68  3.93x chance
  mixing  7.943 m3 [0, 13.52]       4.442 m3 [0, 17]  36.95 min  +0.70  4.06x chance

best: plug flow through 7.943 m3, then a mixed volume of 4.442 m3: 36.95 min on average at the mean flow
plot: runs/<run_id>/lag-profile.png
plot: runs/<run_id>/lag-fit.png

The run folder gets two plots: lag-profile.png, each model's correlation over the delay it tries, and lag-fit.png, the effect with the cause moved by each fitted model.

Three ways material travels¶

Model Parameters The delay is Right when
fixed a dead time constant the throughput is steady
flow a dead volume the volume over the throughput, so it changes with the flow material moves in plug flow, as in a pipe
mixing a dead volume and a mixed volume plug flow, then a first-order spread whose time constant is the mixed volume over the throughput the line has a stirred vessel, or back-mixing

The flow and mixing models count the delay in volume instead of time ("material time"): the cumulative throughput of the --flow tags, summed. A change at the cause reaches the effect when the dead volume has flowed past, however long that takes. Volumes are in the flow tag's unit times hours (m3 for a flow in m3/h; --flow-unit names it). Without --flow, the mixing model works in time instead: a dead time, then a time constant.

The delay column is each model's mean delay in minutes at the mean flow: the dead part plus the mixing part, which is the number to compare with a dead time on the plant graph.

How the delay is found¶

  1. The cause and the flows are resampled to an even grid, by default the cause's median sample period.
  2. Each model tries every delay up to --max-lag-minutes (240), and the mixing model a range of mixed volumes for each: the cause is spread by the mixing, shifted by the dead part, and read at the effect's times.
  3. The prediction is compared with the effect by correlation:
    • An online effect is compared on the grid after prewhitening both with one AR model of the cause, as in graph lags, so that slow drifts do not pass for a link. A peak counts when |corr|·√n reaches --z-threshold (5).
    • A lab effect is compared at its sample times. The chance level comes from running the same search on the cause rotated by a random offset, 59 times per model: rotation cuts the link but keeps the cause's own slow swings. A peak counts when it beats every rotated search of every model (the strength column is the ratio).
  4. A coarse scan finds the peak, and a finer one around it gives the estimate. The interval is the run of values whose |corr| is within two standard errors of the peak's.
  5. The result names the fixed or the flow model, whichever correlates more, unless the mixing model's extra parameter explains significantly more of the effect (an F test on r², p ≈ 0.01).

Checked on Homeostat plants¶

scripts/validate_lag.py runs the bench plant with no fault and no grade change, its feed flows swinging ±10% within the hour, and the reactor's back-mixing drawn per plant, so the true volumes are known. It fits feed B's flow (FT-102) against the lab's product property, with the total flow FT-101 + FT-102: the change crosses the mixer's outlet pipe and the reactor in plug flow, then the reactor's back-mixing spreads it.

With a lab result every hour over 20 days (about 480 results), on six plants:

The mixing model finds the dead volume within 0.25 m3 on average and the mixed volume within 0.5 m3, and the mean delay within 1.7 minutes; the fixed model's delay is off by 3.5 minutes on average, and by 14 minutes on plant 0, the plant with the most mixing, where the noisier data leave the choice at the fixed model. The intervals are wide (the dead volume's often starts at 0), because a dead volume and a mixed volume trade off against each other when the data are sparse. The example at the top of this page is plant 2.

With the usual lab rhythm, a result every 4 hours, 60 days give about 360 results. The mixing model is chosen on five of the six plants, and its mean delay is off by 2.1 minutes on average (dead volume 0.9 m3, mixed volume 1.1 m3); the fixed model's by 4.3 minutes. Three days of 4-hourly results (18 of them) are too few: under 20 results the command stops with exit code 3. A lag from lab data needs weeks.

The script also fits FT-102 against the online analyzer AT-101 on the mixer's outlet. There the analyzer's own reporting delay and sample hold (about 11 minutes, at any flow) blur the transport, and the models agree within about 2 minutes of the true mean delay.

uv sync --extra bench
uv run python scripts/validate_lag.py --plants 6 --days 20 --lab-hours 1
uv run python scripts/validate_lag.py --plants 6 --days 60 --lab-hours 4

Limits¶

  • A lag can only be read where the cause moves. A tag held still by a controller, or a feed that never changes, carries no delay to find, and the result says no model is above chance.
  • Lab results are sparse: weeks of them are needed, and the chance level is honest about it. Grade changes and other large steps dominate a correlation of levels, so pick a window of steady operation with --start and --end.
  • The models are for transport. A link through a controller, a recycle or a heat balance has a delay too, but it is not a volume, and the mixing model may fit it with a misleading one.
  • Several causes moving together share their delays: the fit finds the delay of whatever moves the effect, which need not be the tag named.
